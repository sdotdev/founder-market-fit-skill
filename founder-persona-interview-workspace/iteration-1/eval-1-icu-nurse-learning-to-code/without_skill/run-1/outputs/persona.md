# Founder Persona — Marcus Abel

## Snapshot
Marcus Abel is a 7-year UCSF ICU nurse (3 years as charge nurse) who left clinical work a year ago to teach himself to code and build a tool for ICU shift-change handoffs. He is a deep domain insider on a specific, under-digitized workflow (SBAR handoffs, "brain sheets", EHR gaps), motivated by a patient-safety event he personally witnessed, but he is a technical and commercial outsider: tutorial-level coder, no buyer network, no insider visibility outside his unit.

He is a classic mission-driven, single-problem operator. The shape of the risk in this profile is not motivation or domain credibility — it is shipping capability and go-to-market access. Both should be resolved via co-founder or early hire, not by Marcus spending 12 more months learning.

## Archetype
**Primary: Hound** (deep, specific problem obsession — has been hunting ICU handoff failure for years, has lived the pain, can recite the literature and the workflow anthropology).
**Secondary: Hustler-in-waiting** (charge-nurse operator experience — ran surge events, mediated physician/nurse conflicts, managed cross-functional handoffs with bed control and pharmacy — but this has not been tested against external buyers or capital).
**Weak: Hacker.** Six months of Codecademy plus a tutorial React/Flask to-do app is not a founding-engineer profile.

Archetype mix (rough weights): Hound 0.55, Hustler 0.30, Hacker 0.15.

## Edges

### Market Edge — High (8–9/10)
Marcus has the kind of arcane, insider-only domain detail that is very hard to fake:
- The 5-minute SBAR that "is routinely 20" and why (EHR doesn't surface last vasopressor change, ventilator settings evolution, family communication status).
- The widespread use of paper "brain sheets" that nurses throw away at shift-end — i.e. the real workflow exists outside the system of record.
- Structural contrast between trauma ICU rounding (single-provider) and cardiac ICU rounding (multidisciplinary, politically fraught around extubation).
- Familiarity with Epic Rover as the incumbent mobile surface.
- Citation of Joint Commission sentinel-event data (communication failures underlying 60–70% of adverse events).

This is evidence of real embedded expertise, not reading. Depth probe: passed.

### Technical Edge — Low (1–2/10)
Self-reported honestly: 6 months of Codecademy, one tutorial-followed React + Flask to-do app, and an explicit "I couldn't ship an MVP alone in 72 hours." No shipped production systems, no portfolio, no systems thinking signals. This is not a weakness to hide; it is the single biggest shipping-risk in this profile. **Depth probe: not passed** on technical — flag with calibration note.

### Catalyst Edge — Moderate (5–6/10)
Charge nurse during COVID surge is real catalyst-shaped work: coordinating 30+ patient handoffs, mediating physician-nurse conflict, owning bed-control and pharmacy relationships, running the floor through crisis. This is cross-functional leadership under extreme pressure — the same muscle as a startup 0-to-1. However, he has not tested it externally: no fundraising, no hiring, no vendor negotiation, no public speaking, no partnership closing. Internal clinical catalyst ability is probable; founder-grade catalyst ability is untested.

### Experience / Insight — High (9/10)
7 years at one of the strongest academic medical centers in the country, 3 as charge nurse. He has felt the pain he wants to solve, personally and repeatedly. He saw a patient die from a handoff miss in 2022 and has not been able to put it down since.

### Network — Low (3–4/10)
- 3 private Slack groups of ex-ICU nurses (~200 people combined). Lurker, not active.
- Zero warm intros to hospital administrators, CIOs/CMIOs, VPs of nursing, IT procurement, or healthcare investors.
- Knows floor nurses, who are *users* in a handoff tool but not *buyers*.
- Cold-start on every purchasing vertical in US healthcare.

### Obsession Signals — Very High (specificity 5/5)
- 9+ months reading every ICU-handoff-error paper he can find.
- Can cite Joint Commission sentinel event findings from memory.
- 4 months of nightly wireframe sketches.
- Bought the domain.
- Left a stable career to do this.
- Self-reported: "I'd build this even if no one paid me."

## Motivations
**High missionary (9–10/10).** Anchored to a specific identified patient-safety failure he witnessed. Does not care about exit size. Explicitly said he cares about the problem, not the outcome.

**Low mercenary (2–3/10).** No stated interest in exit window, valuation, category size, or payout structure. This is both an asset (won't flinch under hard quarters) and a risk (may refuse pivots, may underprice, may resist VC-scale growth pressure).

## Stage Fit
**0-to-1, strongly.** Charge-nurse temperament is crisis coordination, not steady-state optimization. The obsession is pre-product. The technical gap means he shouldn't be near a scaling org yet anyway. He should be in a garage with a technical co-founder and a design partner hospital, not running a 30-person company.

## Constraints
- 40–50 hrs/week available (per-diem shifts pay bills).
- 6 months runway — **tight**. This is the binding constraint. Decisions about co-founder, first design partner, and funding need to happen in the first 8–10 weeks of that runway, not the last.
- Bay Area (good for healthcare tech — UCSF, Stanford, Commure, Notable, Abridge, Innovaccer presence; access to a16z Bio+Health, GV, OMERS, Bessemer healthcare teams).
- No dependents, no non-competes, no regulatory issues — clean.

## Credibility Flags / Calibration Notes
1. **Technical Edge flagged.** Self-claim of "could build an MVP with a co-founder" is plausible as a product-minded collaborator, but tutorial-level skill will not survive healthcare's integration complexity (Epic/Cerner APIs, FHIR, HL7, SMART on FHIR, HIPAA BAA, SOC2, hospital IT review cycles). Needs a senior engineer co-founder, not a contractor.
2. **Network flagged.** Knows users, does not know buyers. Hospital software purchasing is one of the hardest B2B motions in existence — committee sale involving CMIO, CNIO, CIO, compliance, nursing leadership, finance, and sometimes a physician champion. Marcus's current network does not touch any of these roles.
3. **No insider markers.** No talks, no publications, no public profile. Solvable — submit to AMIA, HIMSS nursing informatics tracks, write publicly on the same Substack-shaped channel Reyes used; 6 months of public writing plus a design-partner pilot would close a lot of this.
4. **Emotional anchor risk (minor).** Building from grief is a real and valid engine but can make founders rigid about the specific form of the solution. Watch for "the hospital rejected our handoff tool because they want it embedded in Epic, not standalone" and make sure Marcus can hear that.
5. **Runway-vs-sales-cycle mismatch.** 6 months of runway vs. 9–18 month hospital procurement cycles. He cannot self-fund to a paid contract. He needs either (a) angel/pre-seed capital on the strength of problem clarity + co-founder + design partner LOI, or (b) a paid pilot via an innovation-budget / CNIO sponsor route, which is faster but still not 6-month-fast.

## Recommended Next Moves (for the founder, not for evaluation)
- Find a senior full-stack engineer co-founder with healthcare or regulated-industry background. This is the #1 unlock.
- Land one design-partner unit — ideally his old UCSF ICU or a sister unit — as a formal pilot, not a favor.
- Start publishing. One post a week on ICU handoff failure, with data. Builds insider markers and inbound from the exact buyer network he lacks.
- Raise a small pre-seed ($500k–$1.5M) on domain + co-founder + LOI, specifically to extend runway past the first procurement cycle.
- Meet 3 healthcare-focused operators-turned-investors in the Bay within 30 days (warm-intro via ex-ICU nurse Slack — there are ex-clinicians in healthtech ops who can bridge).

## Summary
Marcus is the strongest possible version of a domain-first, mission-driven solo founder: real pain, real expertise, real obsession, clean constraints. He is also, today, un-shippable alone and commercially un-wired. The persona's honest value to him is to name both truths at once: the edge is earned and rare; the gaps are real and need co-founders, not courage.
