# Business model → founder alignment

From `main.md`'s business-model-to-founder table. For each recommended market, pair it with the revenue model most aligned to the founder's archetype. The alignment matters because a founder's "professional language" (MRR/Churn vs GMV/Match-Rate vs Gross Margin) shapes how they'll think about traction, pricing, and growth.

## The four (plus one) revenue models

### Subscription / SaaS
**Professional language:** MRR, ARR, churn rate, net revenue retention, expansion.

**Strategic requirement:** predictability, high LTV:CAC, long-term customer loyalty, continuous product improvement to prevent churn.

**Best founder fit:**
- **Product-Led / Hacker** archetype dominant.
- Obsession signals in product/craft.
- Hipster sensibilities for onboarding and retention.

**Misfits:** pure sales-led Hustlers who optimise for deal size; they tend to under-invest in retention.

### Transactional / e-commerce / fee-per-action
**Professional language:** gross margin, conversion rate, customer acquisition cost (CAC), order value.

**Strategic requirement:** high-volume sales, operational efficiency, distribution mastery.

**Best founder fit:**
- **Sales-Led / Hustler** archetype.
- Sometimes Hipster if the product depends on brand/taste.
- Catalyst edge helps run the ops machine.

**Misfits:** Technical-Edge-heavy founders without distribution instinct.

### Usage-based / consumption
**Professional language:** API calls, compute time, data volume, unit-of-value profitability.

**Strategic requirement:** tying cost directly to customer outcomes, deep understanding of the unit economics.

**Best founder fit:**
- **Technical founders** with differentiated expertise (AWS-shaped, Stripe-shaped).
- Domain-expert founders who understand "what the customer is actually consuming."
- Dev-community insider markers required if the buyer is developers.

**Misfits:** Hustler-only founders who can't reason about the unit economics.

### Marketplace / take-rate
**Professional language:** GMV, match rate, take-rate, liquidity zones, supply/demand density.

**Strategic requirement:** managing two-sided liquidity, fragmented buyer + seller bases, operational coordination.

**Best founder fit:**
- **Catalyst** archetype + **Hustler** tendencies.
- Founders who've run two-sided projects before (conferences, community-building, platform teams).
- Market Edge helps navigate the specific friction of the vertical.

**Misfits:** pure-technical founders — marketplace is 80% operations and 20% product.

### Contract/enterprise/services (additional)
**Professional language:** ACV, TCV, deal cycle, logo count, pilot-to-contract conversion.

**Strategic requirement:** strategic selling, trust signalling, procurement navigation.

**Best founder fit:**
- **Hustler** with deep industry network.
- Catalyst Edge to assemble champions and reference customers.
- Market Edge for credibility.

**Misfits:** Hacker-only, especially without a Hustler co-founder plan.

## Rules for recommending a business model

1. **Name the model explicitly** in the recommendation, not vaguely.
2. **Explain why it fits the archetype**, citing specific persona signal (archetype mix, edge, network).
3. **Don't conflate models** — a "subscription + marketplace fee" hybrid is fine, but explain each leg.
4. **Check the persona's obsession language** — if they talk about their substack (Daniela) or their CLI tool (Jamie) in SaaS-flavoured terms, that's a signal for subscription. If they talk about coordination and liquidity, that's marketplace.

## Anti-pattern to flag

Founders who say "we'll figure out monetisation later" often drift toward whichever model is conceptually easiest for them to think about — usually subscription, because it's the most culturally available. If the persona indicates a classic Hustler-sales archetype but the market naturally wants marketplace economics, call that out. Recommending a subscription SaaS in a market that needs marketplace liquidity because the founder "likes SaaS" is a failure mode this skill should prevent.
